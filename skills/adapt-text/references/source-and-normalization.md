# Source disposition and speech surface

Each owned unit ends narrated, explicitly excluded, or unresolved. Keep the source copy and its locators. Platform notices, title pages, publisher matter, contents, dedications, front-matter epigraphs, translator/editor introductions, notes, indexes and apparatus appendices are paratext even when substantive. Genuine argument, headings and embedded quotations or verse remain body text. A title in a paragraph can be a genuine heading; large type on a title page does not establish a chapter. Unknown roles need a source-evidenced scoped decision.

Remove only indexed citation calls and proven print furniture. For `Beauty[4] for the most part`, the indexed `[4]` is removed while `Beauty for the most part` remains. The note's prose receives its own exclusion. Preserve link-adjacent sentence tails. An included note requires explicit placement instructions; never interleave it automatically.

Normalize one paragraph before immediately assigning its beats. Preserve diction, sentence order and quotation words. Expand spoken numbers, dates, Roman numerals, abbreviations and symbols when context settles their reading. A numbered argument may retain its spoken ordinal; a citation number is apparatus. Preserve uncertain OCR and archaic terms, returning the exact ambiguity rather than guessing. Corrections and pronunciation respellings require a precise scoped rule and edit reason.

Make punctuation speakable without adding explanation. Keep verse lines and stanza relationships. Do not convert layout wraps into paragraphs, phonetic spellings into universal replacements, or heightened prose into dialogue. A quotation with a reporting clause remains narrator prose unless source structure or explicit instructions establish another voice: `he answered, “Return tomorrow.”` keeps the attribution with the quotation; an explicit `ALICE: Return tomorrow.` can justify a registered Alice voice while preserving the spoken words or a specifically approved label convention.

## Pronouncing names as whole words

Start with the source spelling and judge the model's natural reading in context. Chatterbox may
accommodate a foreign name but may do so inconsistently; neither respell every unfamiliar name nor
assume a familiar-looking spelling will always work. Use a scoped intervention when the likely
ambiguity or auditioned error warrants it, preferring a conservative English-readable spelling
that produces the intended name as one flowing word.

Never split a name into syllables in spoken text. Do not insert spaces, hyphens, separators,
syllable-by-syllable capitals, IPA or stress notation to coach the model: these can become pauses,
separate words or spelled-out sounds. For example, do not turn `Yaldabaoth` into `Yal-da-ba-oth` or
`Yal da ba oth`. Keep genuine word boundaries in an actual multiword name; this rule forbids adding
boundaries inside a word, not preserving the author's name structure.

For a needed respelling, record the source form, intended whole-word reading, exact joined spoken
form, reason and unit scope in the brief's pronunciation instructions. Keep analytical phonetics,
if useful for review, out of the spoken replacement. Audition that joined form in a complete phrase
and check later occurrences for consistency. A written pronunciation hint is not proof of the
realized pronunciation; unresolved uncertainty remains a rendering watchlist item.

## Normalized results

Results use the supplied schema. A narrated unit has one canonical normalized string, edit reasons and ordered half-open Unicode character spans. Spans begin at zero, meet without gaps or overlaps and end at string length. Derive their offsets with a script. Do not type beat text separately. Whitespace uses one space within each line and explicit newlines for verse. Record nontrivial before/after edits with reasons. Excluded units have a reason and no speech; unresolved units identify the precise defect or question. Context-only units never appear in results.

The brief's exact unit-scoped rules govern exceptional readings. Select only its registered voices. For directed work copy the accepted action's registered six-value vector; for undirected legacy work use an approved brief choice. A missing registry action or necessary legacy choice is an issue for the main agent. Preserve the source kind and heading hierarchy. No summary, invented speakers or unmarked omissions. Narrate editorial anthology headings and user-requested introductions only from their indexed compilation units and explicit brief rules; do not invent announcements during normalization.
