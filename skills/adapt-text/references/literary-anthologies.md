# Literary anthologies

Apply this reference to an editorial selection of separate works or excerpts. Follow the settled
program, source editions, boundaries, and order in `WORKPAD.md`. Keep one narrator, naming style,
and pronunciation policy across the anthology unless the brief specifies otherwise; let delivery
respond to each source's form and argument.

## Chapter identification

Each delivery chapter must identify its selection and author in both its spoken opening heading
and navigation title. Use the title-first convention `Title, by Author`. For an excerpt with its
own title, use `Selection Title, from Work Title, division, by Author`; otherwise use
`Work Title, division, selected passages, by Author`. Include the source work and book, chapter,
sermon, or letter designation needed to locate the selection. Omit inapplicable elements and
avoid repeating a work title. Keep exact scholarly ranges and translation credits in the listening
guide when they would overload speech; the spoken heading must still identify the selected scope.
An editorial thematic label may accompany this identification, but cannot replace it.

Use the established literary name in the selected edition, such as Plato or Seneca; do not invent
abbreviations. Use Anonymous for unknown authorship, and preserve qualifications on traditional
or disputed attributions. A speaker, translator, editor, or compiler is not thereby the author.
Record one name form per author and reuse it even when that author returns later.

For example, an excerpt headed “Sufism” from Al-Ghazali's work becomes
`Sufism, from Deliverance from Error, by Al-Ghazali`. A bare thematic label or `Chapter Three`
does not identify that selection.

Retain editorial anthology headings in the compilation witness before preparation, with their
editorial provenance and links to the selected editions. Preserve internal source headings;
record any redundant opening-title exclusion in the brief while retaining its source unit.
Do not relabel an editorial heading as the original author's words. Bind the delivery start and
title unit to the anthology heading. Narrate that heading once as one heading speech record,
using ordinary number and abbreviation normalization. Navigation must retain the same words,
subject to the existing title-case review. Do not add title words during mastering or overwrite
an admitted bundle; revisions go through preparation and adaptation again.

## Optional editorial introductions

Include brief editorial introductions only when the user requests them. Write one or two
sentences of factual orientation to the selected passage, making the editorial voice clear
(for example, “In this selection…”). Preserve differences between authors; avoid summaries of
the conclusion, invented biographical claims, and instructions telling the listener what to feel.
Freeze the wording and placement in the compilation witness as separately indexed editorial
paratext. Record the user's request and exact inclusion/placement rule in the brief. Keep the
introduction after the spoken title and before the first source-text unit in the same delivery
chapter. Review it separately from source fidelity; its inclusion does not admit other paratext.

## Spoken transitions and rendering handoff

Use the same heading-to-text pause policy as for an ordinary audiobook. Without an introduction,
target two seconds between the end of the spoken heading and the start of source speech. With
an introduction, target two seconds after the heading and two seconds after the introduction.
These are total audible separations, including existing trailing and leading quiet, rather than
two seconds of extra padding. Audition both transitions and record any departure needed for a
natural, clearly separated opening.

Pass the boundary speech IDs and pause targets to `chatterbox`. The renderer must locate their
terminal/initial segments, inspect the existing quiet, and add only the missing native audio
frames through reviewed assembly gaps. Preserve consonants, breath, and decay. Recheck the
realized joins by listening. Keep timing instructions out of spoken strings and script controls;
punctuation, blank lines, SSML, and bracketed pause commands do not establish timed silence.
